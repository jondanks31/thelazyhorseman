ISSUE #06 · THURSDAY 20 AUGUST 2026 · 6 MIN · HOOVES

The hell is a frog anyway?

The squidgy triangle you have been picking mud out of for years without asking a single question about it.

Pick up a hoof. That rubbery V down the middle, the bit that collects half a field and smells like regret by February, is the frog. Depending on which farrier you ask it is a shock absorber, a pump, a grip pad, or something you should leave well alone.

Here is the short version. Every time the hoof loads, the frog squashes and the digital cushion behind it spreads out. That spreading pushes blood back up the leg, which matters in an animal whose heart sits a very long way from its feet. Stand a horse in a stable for eighteen hours and none of it happens.

A frog that never touches the ground shrinks. That is most of the story, and the fix is movement, not another bottle of spray.

Which brings us to the part nobody enjoys. Thrush likes a wet, still foot. Contracted heels like a foot that never spreads. You can buy every purple spray in the shop and you will still be buying it next winter.

Try this on Saturday

Turn a hoof over in daylight and actually look at it, three days running. You are not diagnosing anything. You are learning what your horse's normal looks like, so that in March you can tell the difference. Costs nothing.

Who is writing this

Me and the cob

I am not a vet, a farrier, a physio or a saddle fitter. I am the person who kept nodding along at the yard and then went home and looked it up.

There is one horse in this. A cob, opinionated, entirely certain that the third fence post from the gate has been moved and is not to be trusted. Most weeks the letter starts with something she has done, or something somebody told me about her that turned out to have no basis at all.

That is really the whole idea. Horses come with an enormous amount of received wisdom, handed down yard to yard, and a fair bit of it is sound. The trouble is that the sound bits and the nonsense arrive in exactly the same tone of voice, usually from someone holding a wheelbarrow. Nobody has time to tell them apart at half six in the morning.

So I do it on a Wednesday evening instead. One subject a week, the actual research where there is any, an honest note where there is not, and every source listed at the bottom so you can go and disagree with me properly.

What this is not

It is not veterinary advice, and it never will be. If your horse is unwell, ring your vet. The letter is for the things nobody rings anybody about, like whether the field really needs harrowing or why we all buy the purple spray.

I get things wrong. When that happens I say so the following Thursday, at the top, not tucked away at the bottom.

The best ones come from you

Suggest a topic

What have you been told at the yard that nobody can actually explain? That is the good stuff. Send it over.

It does not need to be a proper question. "Somebody told me you should never let them drink cold water after work and I have never known whether that is real" is a perfect suggestion. So is "why does everyone at my yard do this one thing and none of them can say why".

The things that make the best issues are usually small, specific, and slightly annoying. Rules with no reason attached. Kit everybody buys without knowing what it does. Advice that changes depending on who is holding the haynet.

What happens next

I read all of them. If yours turns into an issue I will say so at the top, by first name only unless you would rather I did not mention you at all. Plenty of them turn into a line in a future letter rather than a whole issue, which is still a win.

One thing I cannot do is answer questions about your own horse. That is your vet's job, and they have actually seen the animal.

Sponsor an issue

The letter exists to check whether things are true. That puts a limit on who can sensibly pay to sit next to it, so it is worth being straight about who fits.

Who tends to fit

People who make the kit

Rugs, boots, haynets, hard feed, supplements, fencing, trailers. Anything a reader already owns three of and has opinions about.

People who turn up at the yard

Farriers, physios, saddle fitters, dentists, trainers, transport. Especially regional ones, because readers want to know who is any good near them.

Yards and services

Livery yards taking bookings, arenas, insurers, competition centres, and anyone building something useful for horse people.

And who does not

Anything sold on a claim it cannot back up. If the product needs the words miracle, detox, or secret to make sense, we are going to have an awkward conversation, because sooner or later the letter will take a proper look at it. Sponsors are told this before they pay, not after.

How it works

One sponsor per issue. Your slot sits inside the letter, written in the same voice as everything around it, clearly marked as paid, and never dressed up as something I found on my own. You see the copy before it goes out and you get the final say on it.

I will not write that I use something when I do not, and I will not recommend a product I have not looked at. That is the whole reason anyone opens the thing on a Thursday, and it is not for sale.

Tell me what you make or do and roughly when you would want a slot, and I will come back with what is free, what it costs, and the current subscriber figures in writing.
